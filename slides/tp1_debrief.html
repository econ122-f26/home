What the class cut

Ten of 12 teams got four of the five; no team got all five. Almost every team found Coffee Mug, Iced Black Tea, and Plain Bagel. Bottled Water was the one most teams missed.

Trap 1: Bottled Water
9 of 12 teams

“Never bought alone” isn’t “drives a sale”

Drip Coffee and Bottled Water look alike: the two biggest sellers, both $2, both with the thinnest margins on the menu. Both are almost never bought alone.

The question that separates them: when it’s in the basket, is a specific high-value item there more often than usual?

One real link, one non-link

Baskets containing... ...that also have a Caramel Macchiato
Daily Drip Coffee 48%
Bottled Water 23%
(all baskets) 21%
  • Drip Coffee baskets are far more likely to include a Macchiato, the most profitable item on the menu.
  • Water baskets include a Macchiato at the usual rate. Water is popular on its own; it doesn’t bring anything else in. So it goes.
  • Raw co-occurrence misleads: Water shows up next to Cold Brew a lot because both are popular, not because one sells the other. Compare against what you’d expect by chance.

A low-margin item earns its place only if you can show what it brings in.

Trap 2: Peppermint Mocha
6 of 12 teams

Weak for ten months, a star for two

  • On the annual total it looks like a cut: 937 units, $3,470 profit.
  • But nearly all of that comes in November and December, when it sells like one of the best items on the menu.
  • Corporate wants room for a seasonal lineup. Peppermint Mocha already is one. Take it off the menu in the off-season and bring it back for the holidays, rather than cutting it outright.

Trap 3: Iced Black Tea’s missing cost
5 of 12 teams

You don’t need a number you don’t have

product_cost.csv has no row for Iced Black Tea. Several teams made up a cost (a median, an average, an online estimate) and then ranked it as if the number were real. One team’s guess of $0.30 kept the tea on the menu.

Revenue rank (1 = lowest) Product Units Revenue
1 Iced Black Tea 846 $2,538
2 Coffee Mug 294 $3,528
3 Peppermint Mocha 937 $5,154
4 Chocolate Chip Cookie 2,860 $7,641
  • It’s last on revenue, and cost can only subtract from revenue.
  • Even at a cost of $0, its profit would be only $2,540, still among the five lowest earners.
  • So it’s a cut without knowing its cost at all. If you do fill in a number, say so and show the answer doesn’t depend on it.

Trap 4: Cutting Daily Drip Coffee
3 of 12 teams

The worst margin on the menu, and still a keep

  • Drip Coffee earns $0.30 a cup: $2,080 for the whole year, the lowest profit on the menu. Every margin ranking puts it last.
  • But 48% of Drip Coffee baskets also contain a Caramel Macchiato, the top earner at $20,090 a year.
  • If cutting Drip Coffee cost just 10% of Macchiato sales, the shop would lose more than Drip Coffee earns.
  • That’s a loss leader: it brings customers in the door for something else.

A ranking tells you where to look, not what to conclude.

Trap 5: Almond Croissant’s discounts
3 of 12 teams

Count the discounts before you rank

At list price the croissant earns about $7,110. Once its four ~50%-off months are counted, it’s about $4,390, dropping from #11 to #5 of 14 (1 = lowest). The discounts that drove its volume are also what wiped out its profit.

Trap 6: Plain Bagel’s annual total
2 of 12 teams

Annual totals hide time

On its full-year total Plain Bagel looks healthy, mid-pack on every measure. But it collapses in July and stays down, the month Everything Bagel launches. The annual total describes a product that no longer sells.

Was the bagel drop just seasonal?

A summer dip would hit the whole bakery case. It didn’t:

The other bakery items barely move. Plain Bagel’s lost sales show up in Everything Bagel, so demand moved to another product; it didn’t disappear.

Trap 7: Cold Brew’s duplicate cost rows
0 of 12 teams

Check the join before you trust it

c(rows = nrow(product_cost), products = n_distinct(product_cost$product_id))
##     rows products 
##       16       14

product_info %>% anti_join(product_cost, by = "product_id") %>% select(product_name)
## # A tibble: 1 × 1
##   product_name  
##   <chr>         
## 1 Iced Black Tea

product_cost %>% count(product_id) %>% filter(n > 1)
## # A tibble: 1 × 2
##   product_id     n
##   <chr>      <int>
## 1 P09            3

16 rows, but only 14 products for a 15-item menu. nrow() alone makes it look like one stray extra row. anti_join() and count() show the two real problems: Iced Black Tea is missing, and Cold Brew is listed three times. Joined as-is, Cold Brew’s sales triple. Every team caught this.

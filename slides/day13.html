Ask the neighbors

We’re ordering a T-shirt for a new student: 176 cm, 75 kg. Medium or Large?

  • Each dot: a past student and the size that fit them.
  • 5 nearest: 3 Large, 2 Medium → P(Large) = 3/5 → predict Large.

What we just did: the KNN classifier

The same three steps work for any new case and any number of predictors.

  1. Measure the distance from the new case to every training case. For the new student: \(d = \sqrt{(\text{height} - 176)^2 + (\text{weight} - 75)^2}\). More predictors, more terms.
  2. Take the \(K\) closest. (We used \(K = 5\).)
  3. Vote: P(Large) = the share of those \(K\) that are Large (3/5). Predict the majority.

In R, from the class package:

knn(train = train_X, test = test_X, cl = train_y, k = 5)

Parametric vs non-parametric

Logistic regression KNN
Assumes a shape? Yes: the S-curve, linear in z No
Optimizes? Yes: the β’s, by maximum likelihood No. Nothing to fit
We choose The cutoff K and the cutoff
Keeps, to predict Just β₀, β₁, … The whole training set
Flexibility Set by the terms we include Set by K
Explain a prediction? Yes, with the coefficients Only “similar cases were Yes”
  • Parametric: estimate a fixed set of parameters. Non-parametric: let the data set the shape.
  • Day 9’s table: KNN sits at the flexible, hard-to-interpret end.

Training vs test error

For KNN, choosing \(K\) by training error is especially bad. Why?

  • Test error is lowest at \(K =\) 15: Day 9’s U-shape again.

2. Distance needs a common scale

Same students, different units

Record height in meters, not cm. Should the new student’s T-shirt change?

  • In meters, heights differ by only ~0.1, so weight alone picks the neighbors.
  • Standardize first (subtract the mean, divide by the SD; scale()). Then cm or m gives the same answer.

The same comparison on the loans

Why don’t the loans reward KNN’s flexibility?

  • Both put the boundary near $2,000: Day 11’s rule, found from the data.
  • KNN’s wiggles add variance, not accuracy. When the two are close, prefer logistic: it’s more stable, and it explains itself.

Summary

KNN classifies a new case by a vote of its \(K\) nearest training cases.

  • Non-parametric: no assumed shape, no coefficients. Flexibility comes from \(K\).
  • Small \(K\): flexible, low bias, high variance. \(K = 1\) has 0% training error and can lose to the baseline on new data.
  • Large \(K\): rigid, high bias, low variance. Very large \(K\) just predicts the majority class.
  • Choose \(K\) by test error, never training error.
  • Standardize the predictors, or the one with the biggest spread decides who counts as a neighbor.
